## Original design reference

### Ink Wash Visual Style

## 1. Style Definition

The visual style of this project is:

**Modern Ink Wash**

It combines modern minimal interface design with the visual atmosphere of traditional Chinese ink painting.

The design should feel:

- calm
- poetic
- restrained
- natural
- elegant
- quiet
- breathable
- refined

The style should reference Chinese ink painting indirectly through:

- color
- texture
- contrast
- whitespace
- brush rhythm
- natural forms

Do not imitate traditional painting literally.

The final result should still feel contemporary.

---

## 2. Core Principle

The guiding principle is:

> Modern form, ink-wash spirit.

The design should not feel ancient, theatrical, or decorative-heavy.

Traditional influence should appear subtly.

Prefer atmosphere over ornament.

---

## 3. Visual Keywords

Primary keywords:

- ink
- paper
- mist
- stone
- bamboo
- seal
- brush
- silence
- emptiness
- balance
- natural rhythm

Emotional qualities:

- calm
- elegant
- restrained
- warm
- contemplative
- timeless

---

## 4. Color Philosophy

The palette should be based on natural, desaturated tones.

Primary visual colors:

- warm paper white
- ink black
- charcoal gray
- stone gray
- muted brown
- seal red
- bamboo green
- mist blue-gray

Avoid highly saturated colors.

Color should feel slightly aged, organic, and soft.

## 6. Contrast

Contrast should be soft but readable.

Avoid:

- pure black on pure white
- extremely harsh borders
- strong neon contrast

Prefer:

```text
warm paper
+
soft black ink
```

instead of:

```text
#FFFFFF
+
#000000
```

Primary text should remain clearly readable.

---

## 7. Whitespace

Whitespace is a core part of the style.

Whitespace should feel intentional.

Use empty space to create:

- calm
- hierarchy
- rhythm
- focus

Do not fill every empty area with decoration.

In ink painting, empty space is part of the composition.

The same principle applies here.

---

## 8. Composition

Composition should feel asymmetrical but balanced.

Prefer:

- visually weighted empty space
- subtle imbalance
- organic rhythm
- deliberate negative space

Avoid rigid visual symmetry everywhere.

The layout should feel composed rather than mechanically centered.

---

## 9. Typography

Typography should be elegant and understated.

Primary interface typography should remain modern and highly readable.

Suitable directions:

- modern sans-serif
- clean humanist sans-serif
- restrained serif for selected headings

Chinese typography may use:

- Source Han Sans
- Source Han Serif
- Noto Sans CJK
- Noto Serif CJK

---

## 10. Calligraphy

Calligraphy fonts are decorative elements only.

Use them for:

- branding
- short titles
- visual signatures
- artistic labels

Do not use calligraphy fonts for:

- body text
- long paragraphs
- controls
- menus
- data
- small text

Calligraphy should function like artwork, not ordinary typography.

---

## 11. Typography Weight

Prefer:

- Regular
- Medium
- Semibold

Avoid excessive bold typography.

Strong weight should only appear where hierarchy requires it.

The visual tone should feel light rather than heavy.

---

## 12. Borders

Borders should feel subtle.

Prefer:

- thin strokes
- warm gray
- low contrast

Borders should resemble light ink lines rather than strong geometric outlines.

Avoid:

- thick black borders
- heavy boxes
- borders around every element

---

## 13. Corner Radius

Corners should be restrained.

Recommended visual character:

- mostly subtle rounding
- sometimes near-square

Typical radius range:

```text
2px
4px
6px
8px
```

Avoid very large rounded corners.

The style should not resemble a playful mobile UI.

---

## 14. Shapes

Prefer simple geometric shapes softened by subtle organic details.

Suitable shapes:

- rectangle
- rounded rectangle
- circle
- thin line
- irregular brush stroke

Organic shapes should be decorative, not structural.

---

## 15. Shadows

Shadows should be extremely subtle.

Use them only when depth is necessary.

Preferred shadow character:

- soft
- diffuse
- low-opacity
- neutral or warm

Avoid:

- strong floating shadows
- dramatic elevation
- glowing shadows

Ink Wash style should feel mostly flat.

---

## 16. Surface Style

Surfaces should feel similar to:

- paper
- parchment
- stone
- matte ceramic

Avoid surfaces that feel:

- glossy
- plastic
- metallic
- glass-like

The style should feel tactile but understated.

---

## 17. Texture

Texture is optional.

Suitable texture references:

- Xuan paper
- handmade paper
- dry brush
- subtle ink bleed
- stone grain

Texture must remain very low contrast.

Good texture should almost disappear when not actively noticed.

---

## 18. Paper Texture

Paper texture should be:

- soft
- subtle
- irregular
- low frequency

Avoid obvious repeating texture patterns.

Do not use heavy paper fibers behind small text.

When uncertain, use a plain warm background instead.

---

## 19. Ink Texture

Ink texture may appear as:

- soft edges
- subtle bleed
- dry-brush detail
- diluted gray washes

Do not use realistic ink effects on every object.

Ink effects should mainly appear in decorative areas.

---

## 20. Brush Strokes

Brush strokes are accent elements.

Suitable uses:

- short dividers
- heading underline
- visual highlight
- decorative frame fragment
- illustration detail

Avoid using brush strokes everywhere.

A few strokes are stronger than many.

---

## 21. Ink Wash

Ink wash gradients should feel organic.

Suitable transitions:

```text
Ink Gray → Transparent
```

or

```text
Mist Gray → Paper
```

Edges should feel soft and irregular.

Avoid digital-looking linear gradients.

---

## 22. Gradient Rules

Conventional colorful gradients should generally be avoided.

Avoid:

- blue-purple gradient
- orange-pink gradient
- neon gradients
- rainbow gradients

Allowed:

- subtle tonal ink wash
- mist-like opacity fade
- monochrome wash

---

## 23. Decorative Motifs

Suitable motifs include:

- mountains
- mist
- bamboo
- stone
- plum blossom
- water
- clouds
- seal marks

Use motifs sparingly.

They should support atmosphere, not dominate the composition.

---

## 24. Seal Motif

Seal red is one of the signature accents of this style.

Seal shapes may be:

- square
- rectangular
- slightly irregular

Use them for:

- branding
- small labels
- signatures
- key visual accents

Avoid placing seals everywhere.

One seal can establish the style.

---

## 25. Illustration Style

Illustrations should use:

- monochrome ink
- muted colors
- large empty areas
- simplified forms
- subtle brush textures

Avoid:

- glossy 3D renders
- cartoon vector characters
- highly saturated illustrations
- dense anime-style imagery

---

## 26. Icon Style

Icons should be:

- simple
- thin
- geometric
- restrained

Preferred characteristics:

- consistent stroke width
- low visual complexity
- minimal fill

Avoid mixing:

- filled icons
- outline icons
- cartoon icons
- hand-painted icons

unless intentionally designed as one coherent system.

---

## 27. Motion

Motion should feel calm.

Suitable motion qualities:

- slow fade
- gentle reveal
- subtle slide
- opacity transition
- soft expansion

Avoid:

- bounce
- elastic spring
- aggressive zoom
- spinning decoration
- energetic motion

---

## 28. Motion Inspiration

Motion may evoke:

- mist appearing
- ink spreading
- paper unfolding
- water settling

Do not literally simulate these effects unless they remain subtle.

The result should still feel like normal modern UI animation.

---

## 29. Animation Timing

Recommended range:

```text
80–150ms
```

for small interactions.

```text
150–300ms
```

for larger transitions.

Avoid long ornamental animations.

---

## 30. Hover Style

Hover should remain subtle.

Possible changes:

- slight darkening
- slight paper-tone shift
- border strength increase
- opacity increase

Avoid:

- glow
- strong scaling
- dramatic color shift

---

## 31. Selected State

Selected states may use:

- seal red
- deeper ink
- subtle tint
- thin accent line

Selection should remain elegant and clear.

---

## 32. Visual Density

The style should feel breathable.

However, breathable does not mean oversized.

Avoid:

- giant spacing everywhere
- oversized controls
- excessive empty vertical gaps

Use whitespace deliberately.

---

## 33. Layering

Visual hierarchy should primarily come from:

1. whitespace
2. typography
3. tone
4. subtle borders
5. restrained accent color

Avoid depending heavily on shadows.

---

## 34. Photography

If photography is used, prefer:

- desaturated imagery
- natural light
- subdued contrast
- simple composition

Photos may be lightly toned toward:

- warm gray
- brown
- muted green

Avoid vivid commercial photography.

---

## 35. Material Character

The overall visual material should feel:

```text
paper
ink
stone
wood
ceramic
```

rather than:

```text
glass
chrome
plastic
neon
```

---

## 36. Light Theme

The default style is light.

Light theme should resemble:

> Ink placed on warm paper.

Use warm off-white backgrounds.

Avoid cold pure-white interfaces.

---

## 37. Dark Theme

Dark theme should resemble:

> Pale paper and seal colors emerging from deep ink.

Suggested background direction:

```text
#1C1A18
#24211E
#2C2824
```

Text should be warm off-white.

Avoid pure black backgrounds.

---

## 38. Style Balance

The design should roughly feel:

```text
80% modern minimalism
20% traditional ink influence
```

Do not reverse this balance.

The traditional style should be felt, not constantly displayed.

---

## 39. Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- glassmorphism
- cyberpunk
- neon
- glossy surfaces
- excessive gradients
- giant rounded cards
- overly playful UI
- excessive shadows
- bright SaaS palette
- heavy Chinese ornament
- gold borders everywhere
- traditional pattern on every surface
- calligraphy everywhere
- large ink splashes behind text

---

## 40. Important Rule

Do not confuse:

**Chinese style**

with:

**putting traditional motifs everywhere.**

The style should come primarily from:

- restraint
- color
- whitespace
- rhythm
- material
- typography
- atmosphere

not decoration.

---

## 41. Visual Reference Formula

When creating a new visual element, use this formula:

```text
Modern Minimalism
+
Warm Paper
+
Soft Ink
+
Large Negative Space
+
One Restrained Traditional Detail
```

Do not add multiple decorative ideas at once.

---

## 42. Final Visual Test

Before accepting a design, ask:

### Does it feel calm?

If no, reduce color and decoration.

### Does it feel modern?

If no, remove literal traditional ornament.

### Does it feel Eastern without being theatrical?

If yes, the balance is correct.

### Is the decoration more noticeable than the content?

If yes, reduce the decoration.

### Does every visual element have a purpose?

If no, remove it.

---

## Final Principle

The desired result is not:

> an ancient Chinese interface.

The desired result is:

> a modern interface with the visual temperament of Chinese ink painting.

### Archived UI Design

All UI implementation and modification MUST follow `docs/reference/DESIGN.md`.

The user's 2026-10-22 Neobrutalism redesign request replaces the ink-wash visual
direction below. Follow the current-direction section at the top of `docs/reference/DESIGN.md`
and https://github.com/neobrutalism/neobrutalism for the active UI. Continue to
apply the reusable-component, layout, accessibility and review rules below.

The project uses a **modern Chinese ink-wash inspired design language**.

The design is not intended to imitate an ancient Chinese interface literally.

The goal is:

> Modern desktop application structure with the quiet, restrained, natural feeling of Chinese ink painting.

Usability and readability always take priority over decoration.

---

### Core UI Principles

Every UI change should be:

- calm
- restrained
- readable
- consistent
- spacious
- functional
- desktop-oriented

Prefer subtle traditional Chinese visual cues instead of obvious decorative styling.

The interface should feel like:

- Xuan paper
- ink
- mist
- brush rhythm
- stone
- bamboo
- seal red

without becoming an illustration or poster.

---

### Before Editing UI

Before creating or modifying UI:

1. Read `docs/reference/DESIGN.md`.
2. Inspect nearby existing UI components.
3. Reuse existing components whenever possible.
4. Reuse existing design tokens.
5. Preserve the existing visual hierarchy.
6. Do not redesign unrelated UI.

Do not introduce a new visual pattern for a single feature.

If a new pattern is necessary, make it reusable.

---

### Slint Component Rules

Prefer reusable Slint components.

Examples:

- `AppButton`
- `IconButton`
- `ToolButton`
- `TextField`
- `NumberField`
- `CheckBox`
- `Panel`
- `Section`
- `Sidebar`
- `Toolbar`
- `StatusBar`
- `Dialog`
- `Tab`
- `PropertyRow`
- `Divider`

Do not repeatedly implement identical styling in different files.

Bad:

```slint
Rectangle {
    background: #f5f0e8;
    border-color: #d4ccc0;
    border-width: 1px;
    border-radius: 6px;
}
```

copied into many components.

Prefer reusable components or theme tokens.

---

### Design Tokens

Colors, spacing, control sizes, radii, and typography SHOULD come from centralized design tokens.

Prefer:

```slint
global Theme {
    // colors
    in property <color> background;
    in property <color> surface;
    in property <color> text-primary;
    in property <color> text-secondary;
    in property <color> border;
    in property <color> accent;

    // spacing
    in property <length> spacing-xs;
    in property <length> spacing-sm;
    in property <length> spacing-md;
    in property <length> spacing-lg;

    // geometry
    in property <length> radius-sm;
    in property <length> radius-md;
    in property <length> control-height;
}
```

Do not hard-code arbitrary visual values throughout the project.

---

### Layout Rules

Use Slint layout components whenever possible:

- `VerticalLayout`
- `HorizontalLayout`
- `GridLayout`

Avoid absolute positioning for normal UI.

Absolute positioning is acceptable for:

- canvas overlays
- CAD overlays
- floating tool controls
- drag handles
- visual annotations
- coordinate-based content

Normal forms and panels should use layouts.

---

### Spacing

Use the project spacing scale.

Preferred values:

- 4px
- 8px
- 12px
- 16px
- 24px
- 32px

Do not casually introduce values such as:

- 7px
- 13px
- 19px
- 27px

unless required for precise visual alignment.

---

### Interaction States

Interactive components should define appropriate states.

When applicable, include:

- normal
- hover
- pressed
- selected
- focused
- disabled

Do not rely on color alone when the interaction state would otherwise be unclear.

Active tools and selected items must be immediately recognizable.

---

### Animation

Animations must be subtle and functional.

Prefer short transitions.

Typical durations:

- hover: 100–140ms
- pressed: 80–120ms
- selection: 120–160ms
- panel transition: 160–220ms
- dialog transition: 160–220ms

Avoid:

- bounce
- elastic motion
- strong scaling
- continuous decorative animation
- unnecessary movement

For precision-oriented interfaces such as CAD or editors, avoid animation that can interfere with pointer accuracy.

---

### Typography

Normal application text must prioritize readability.

Do not use decorative calligraphy fonts for:

- body text
- forms
- menus
- toolbars
- property panels
- numeric values

Calligraphy-inspired typography may only be used sparingly for:

- welcome screens
- decorative headings
- branding

UI text should remain modern and readable.

---

### Color Rules

Use semantic theme colors.

Do not scatter raw color literals throughout UI files.

Prefer tokens such as:

- `background`
- `surface`
- `surface-hover`
- `surface-active`
- `border`
- `border-strong`
- `text-primary`
- `text-secondary`
- `text-disabled`
- `accent`
- `accent-hover`
- `danger`
- `warning`
- `success`

Accent colors should be used sparingly.

Seal red must not dominate the application.

---

### Water-Ink Decoration

Ink-wash decoration is optional and must remain subtle.

Allowed:

- faint paper texture
- subtle ink gradient in large empty areas
- small seal-red mark
- restrained brush-line divider
- low-opacity landscape silhouette
- soft mist-like decorative background

Do not place decoration behind dense readable content.

Decorative elements must never reduce:

- contrast
- readability
- pointer precision
- information density

---

### Desktop UI Rules

This is a desktop application.

Prefer:

- compact controls
- structured panels
- clear toolbars
- visible state
- medium-to-high information density
- keyboard-friendly interaction

Avoid mobile-oriented styling such as:

- huge touch targets
- giant rounded cards
- oversized headings
- excessive vertical spacing
- pill-shaped everything

---

### Tool / Editor Interfaces

For tool-oriented screens:

- Main content or canvas receives the most visual space.
- Side panels remain compact.
- Toolbars should be visually quiet.
- Selected tools must be obvious.
- Numeric values should align consistently.
- Properties should be grouped logically.
- Do not overuse cards.

Prefer flat panel hierarchy over dashboard-style card grids.

---

### Dialogs

Dialogs should:

- have a clear title
- contain only relevant content
- use consistent internal spacing
- place actions consistently
- clearly distinguish primary and secondary actions

Avoid oversized dialogs.

Avoid decorative backgrounds inside ordinary dialogs.

---

### Icons

Use a consistent icon family.

Prefer simple:

- outline icons
- geometric icons
- restrained stroke icons

Do not use:

- emoji as application icons
- random mixed icon styles
- cartoon icons
- highly ornamental Chinese symbols for ordinary actions

Traditional motifs may be used only where semantically appropriate.

---

### Forbidden Styles

Do NOT introduce the following unless explicitly requested:

- glassmorphism
- neon UI
- cyberpunk styling
- heavy gradients
- strong glow
- excessive shadows
- large pill-shaped controls
- giant SaaS-style cards
- colorful dashboard blocks
- overly playful illustrations
- dense ornamental Chinese borders
- large ink splashes behind content

---

### Do Not Over-Theme

The application is not a poster.

The application is not a traditional painting viewer.

The application is not a historical-game UI.

Ink-wash aesthetics should influence:

- color
- rhythm
- texture
- whitespace
- contrast
- small decorative details

not replace modern interaction patterns.

---

### Accessibility

Maintain sufficient contrast.

Do not use very light gray text on paper-colored backgrounds for important information.

Focused controls must remain identifiable.

Disabled states must remain distinguishable.

Important information must not be communicated through color alone.

---

### Final Review

Before completing a UI task, verify:

- UI follows `docs/reference/DESIGN.md`.
- Existing components were reused where possible.
- No unnecessary new visual pattern was introduced.
- Spacing follows the design scale.
- Colors use semantic theme values.
- Typography remains readable.
- Hover states are present where needed.
- Pressed states are present where needed.
- Selected states are clear.
- Disabled states are clear.
- Alignment is consistent.
- Decorative ink elements remain subtle.
- Main functionality remains visually dominant.
- The result looks like part of the same application.

If the new UI looks more decorative than functional, simplify it.
